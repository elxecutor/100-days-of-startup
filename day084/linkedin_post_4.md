HFSS vs CST vs AntennaForge — an honest comparison from someone who used all three

I've spent thousands of hours in HFSS and CST over my career. And I helped build AntennaForge. So take this with whatever bias you want — but here's my honest take.

🔷 HFSS (Ansys)
Gold standard for precision. The FEM solver handles fine geometric details better than anything else. If you're designing a satellite antenna or a 77 GHz automotive radar module, HFSS is still the right tool.
The catch: $30k+/year, dedicated workstation required, steep learning curve, license management nightmare.

🔷 CST (Dassault)
Excellent broadband solver. The TLM and time-domain solvers are faster than HFSS for many wideband problems. Strong integration with other Dassault tools. Better UI than HFSS, in my opinion.
The catch: Similar pricing to HFSS. Acquired by Dassault — the ecosystem lock-in is real. Still requires local installation and license server.

🔷 AntennaForge
We're not trying to beat HFSS or CST at their own game. We can't — they've had 30+ years of development. What we offer is:
- Zero upfront cost. Pay per simulation or $200/month unlimited.
- No install. Browser-based. Works on a Chromebook.
- Share projects with anyone. No license needed to view.
- Autoscaling cloud compute. Your simulation doesn't tie up your machine.
- We optimized for the 80% use case: PCB antennas at 433 MHz to 6 GHz.

Where we fall short: electrically large structures (reflectors, arrays), niche solver features (hybrid FEBI, eigenmode), and the sheer breadth of material models in legacy tools. We're honest about this.

Who should use what:
→ Satellite/aerospace/automotive radar → HFSS or CST
→ IoT sensor, smart home, wearable, consumer device → AntennaForge (or try us alongside your existing tool)
→ RF consultant working with multiple clients → AntennaForge (the collaboration model is way better)
→ University lab teaching antenna theory → AntennaForge (no per-seat licensing for students)

The RF tool market hasn't changed in 20 years. It's time for options.
